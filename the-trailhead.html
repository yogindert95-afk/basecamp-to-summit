The Trailhead · Campus to corporate · 3 hours

The corporate world
has no syllabus.

Your degree taught you the subject. Nobody taught you the job: how to introduce yourself, answer an interview question with a real example, speak up when you're the newest person in the room, and make a good start. The Trailhead is a 3-hour session that covers exactly that — before your first day, not after your first stumble.

The problem

College rewards knowing the answer. Work rewards something else.

At work, nobody hands you a syllabus. What gets noticed is explaining things clearly, listening, taking feedback well, and finishing what you start without someone checking your homework. Most people learn this by tripping over it.

The Trailhead gives you a head start in one afternoon: four skills, each practised in the room, and three things you take home.

Who this is for

Three people standing at the trailhead.

The final-year student

Placements are coming and your CV says "team player" without an example to back it. You want to walk into interviews with real stories to tell, and into your first job knowing what to do.

The student with an internship coming

Your first weeks in an office are close, and nobody has told you how any of it works. You want to know how to introduce yourself, what to ask, and how to make a good start.

The college or placement cell

Your students are academically strong but employers keep saying "not workplace-ready." We run The Trailhead on campus as one 3-hour session — practical, and shaped to the industries your students enter.

What's covered

Three hours. Four skills. All practised in the room.

No theory to memorise. Each part is a short explanation followed by practice with the person next to you, plus a short break and open questions.

3 hours 4 skills Practice, not lectures 3 things to take home

Open each skill to see what you learn — and what you practise.

1

Speak clearly · 40 min

+

Say what you mean, simply — when you introduce yourself, when you write a message, and when you're asked a question you didn't expect.
What you'll learn
Lead with the point
Start with your answer in one line, then explain. It works in interviews, emails, and meetings.
Introduce yourself in 60 seconds
Who you are, what you've done, and what you're looking for — without reciting your CV.
Write a message people can act on
A clear subject, a clear ask, and nothing the reader has to guess.
You'll practise in the room
  • Give your 60-second introduction to a partner, then improve it.
  • Rewrite one messy message so the ask is obvious.
2

Get interview-ready · 45 min

+

Interviews are a skill you can practise. Learn what the person across the table is looking for, and how to show it with real examples.
What you'll learn
What interviewers are really checking
Can you do the work, will you learn, and would they want you on the team.
Answer with a story
The situation, what you did, and what happened — taken from a project, an internship, or a college event.
Questions worth asking them
What to ask at the end so you sound curious, not rehearsed.
You'll practise in the room
  • Write one story from your own college experience.
  • Tell it to a partner and hear what landed — then swap seats.
3

Work well with others · 35 min

+

Group projects were the warm-up. At work, how you listen, take feedback, and ask for help decides whether people want you on their team.
What you'll learn
Listening properly
The difference between waiting for your turn to talk and actually hearing someone.
Taking feedback without getting defensive
Treat it as information, say thank you, and act on it.
Asking for help early
Why asking on day two beats struggling quietly until the deadline.
You'll practise in the room
  • A listening exercise in pairs, both seats.
  • Practise responding to a piece of tough feedback.
4

Plan your first 90 days · 40 min

+

A new job or internship has no timetable. Arrive with a simple plan for your first three months instead of waiting to be told what to do.
What you'll learn
Your first week
Who to meet, what to ask, and what to write down.
Owning your work
Keep a list, give updates before anyone has to ask, and finish what you start.
One early win
Pick one small thing to do well and visibly in your first month.
You'll practise in the room
  • Write your own one-page first-90-days plan.
  • Share it with a partner and tighten it.
You leave with
  • A 60-second introduction you've already said out loud.
  • One interview story from your own experience, tested on a partner.
  • A one-page plan for your first 90 days.
For colleges & placement cells

How it runs on your campus.

Your students are academically strong. The Trailhead adds what employers say is missing — and it's built around the roles and industries your batch is heading into.

Before: shaped to your batch

We start with a call with your placement cell: who your students are and where they get hired. Optional pre-learning can be shared ahead of the session.

On campus: practice, not lectures

One 3-hour session, half teaching and half practice. In person is recommended; online is available. Facilitation is planned around the size of your batch.

After: something to show

Every student leaves with three things: a 60-second introduction, one interview story of their own, and a one-page first-90-days plan.
